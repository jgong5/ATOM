## Answer: YES — subclassing works. No vendoring. W1.9 stands at 450–650 LOC.

Six claims, all executed and passing, with **zero edits to agentx-harness**:

| # | Claim | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The strategy is built by the plugin factory, not hard-constructed | `PhaseRunner._build_strategy` disassembles to `plugins.get_class(...)`; the class name never appears |
| 2 | An out-of-tree subclass displaces the built-in | `plugins.register(TIMING_STRATEGY, "agentic_replay", cls, priority=100)` → `get_class` returns ours |
| 3 | The wrapper surface is complete | the strategy touches exactly `schedule_later`, `set_drain_observer`, `running_count`, `cap_pending_delay` |
| 4 | `LoopScheduler` is a module global in `timing/phase/runner.py` | `LOAD_GLOBAL` in `PhaseRunner.__init__` — so it is substitutable from our bootstrap |
| 5 | The subclass **constructs** through the real base `__init__` | built with a real `TrajectorySource` + real `LoopScheduler`; `self.scheduler` is our wrapper |
| 6 | (negative) Pacing sites exist **outside** the strategy | `branch_orchestrator.py:1265`, `:1467`, `replay_dependencies.py:319` |

### Why it is this cheap

`scheduler` is a **keyword-only constructor parameter** (`agentic_replay.py:116`), assigned
once at `:142`. The entire override is:

```python
class CompassAgenticReplay(AgenticReplayStrategy):
    def __init__(self, *, scheduler, **kw):
        super().__init__(scheduler=ClockPacedScheduler(scheduler), **kw)
```

Three lines. `ClockPacedScheduler` overrides `schedule_later` and `__getattr__`-forwards
the rest — about 12 lines. `PhaseRunner._build_strategy` passes every argument by keyword,
so `**kw` is future-proof against upstream adding parameters.

## The finding that changes the design — `06` D34 is too narrow

> `06` D34: *"The single line that matters is `timing/strategies/agentic_replay.py:1559`."*

It is not. For `AGENTIC_REPLAY`, `PhaseRunner.__init__` hands the **same** `LoopScheduler`
instance to three more consumers, and **`BranchOrchestrator` is always built** for this
timing mode (`runner.py:267-274` docstring). Their pacing calls are real arrivals:

| Site | What it paces |
|---|---|
| `branch_orchestrator.py:1265` | a spawned child's **turn-0 dispatch** at its recorded offset |
| `branch_orchestrator.py:1467` | the **join replay deadline** that releases a blocked parent |
| `replay_dependencies.py:319` | `cap_pending_delay_for_group` inside `ReplayBarrierCoordinator` |

A strategy subclass wraps `self.scheduler` and **never sees any of these**. Under virtual
time they would advance on the real clock — a silent divergence, not a crash.

**D34's stronger claim survives, and is what saves us:** all of it really does funnel
through `LoopScheduler`. `branch_orchestrator.py:1279` is a raw `await asyncio.sleep(...)`,
but its own docstring says it is the fallback for *"isolated callers without a scheduler"*;
production takes the scheduler branch.

### So the seam is the scheduler, not the strategy

`PhaseRunner`, `LoopScheduler`, `BranchOrchestrator` and `ReplayBarrierCoordinator` are
**not** plugin categories (checked against all 33 in `plugins.yaml`). But claim 4 shows
`LoopScheduler` is resolved as a module global at `runner.py:191`, so our plugin bootstrap
can rebind `aiperf.timing.phase.runner.LoopScheduler` to `ClockPacedScheduler` — one line,
still zero edits to their repo, and every consumer gets it because they all receive the
one instance `PhaseRunner.__init__` builds.

## Escalation to the owner

T10 does **not** fire. W1.9 stays at 450–650 LOC. What needs a decision is which seam:

| | Reaches | Cost | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. subclass the strategy** | 4 of 7 production pacing sites | ~15 LOC | **silently wrong** — three arrival paths stay on the real clock |
| **B. rebind the module global** (recommended) | all 7, one instance | ~5 LOC | depends on an import-time name, not a public API; breaks loudly if upstream inlines it |
| **C. both** | all 7, with the subclass as the assertion point | ~20 LOC | B does the work; A asserts B took effect |

A is what `06` D34 describes and it is the one option that can be quietly wrong.

- **`12`** — close T10 as *answered, did not fire*; open a successor for the B-vs-C choice.

## Handoff to W1.9

1. The override is three lines; the wrapper is twelve. Budget goes to the clock client and
   the transport, not to the strategy.
2. Register at `priority=100` under the **existing** name `agentic_replay` — same-priority
   external already beats built-in, so 100 is belt-and-braces.
3. `PhaseRunner` passes everything by keyword. Keep `**kw` opaque; do not enumerate.
4. Whatever wraps the scheduler must implement `schedule_later`, `schedule_at`,
   `schedule_at_perf_sec`, `schedule_at_perf_ns`, `cap_pending_delay` and
   `cap_pending_delay_for_group` — the last two are advancement, not arming, and a clock
   that ignores them will drift.

---

## Decision (owner, 2026-09-20)

**Option B chosen** — rebind the `LoopScheduler` module global from the plugin bootstrap,
reaching all seven pacing sites rather than the four a strategy subclass sees. A subclass
remains the mechanism by which the rebound class is installed, and the fallback if upstream
stops sharing the instance. Amendments landed at `9120a96a3`: `06` D34 (correction plus the
seven-site table), `12` T10 (resolved), `16` W1.9 (estimate no longer conditional; its
acceptance must assert that none of the seven advances on the real clock).
