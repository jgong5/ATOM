Price prefill from its own graph, and stop pretending it is replayed

--compass-trace-prefill records a prefill step alongside the decode
one, --compass-bench-graph takes both, and the oracle picks by kind.
The calibrated fallback is now unused: ttft -12.5%, tpot -0.1%,
latency -4.7%, and at 96 output tokens -12.9% / -3.5% / -4.9%.

TTFT looks like a regression against the fallback's -1.3% and is not.
That was a calibrated oracle fitted to the steps it was predicting;
this is the op graph answering for the first time. The prefill step
itself is 36.6ms against a measured 37.3 -- -1.9% -- and the rest of
TTFT is admission and scheduling outside any step, which is problem 3.

Three things had to be right and two were wrong first.

Prefill reads more metadata than decode: the varlen fmha kernel wants
cu_seqlens_k and min_seqlen_q too and raises rather than defaulting
them, so recording only what decode needed left all 28 prefill
attentions unpriced -- the whole of prefill's attention cost.
Coverage 94.6% to 98.9%.

A prefill step runs eager and that is most of it. Prefill's kernels
are 15.1ms of a 37.3ms step; the priced sum was right and the model
was wrong. A replayed step is one submission with the host out of the
loop, an eager one dispatches every operator. Two constants: 2.02us
per kernel launch replayed, 67.4us per operator eager. Thirtyfold
apart, which is why the replayed constant priced prefill at 0.42 of
its step.

And the engine has to say which. capture_bucket is documented as None
when nothing was replayed, but _capture_bucket only looked at batch
size, so a four-sequence prefill was reported as replaying the batch-4
decode graph it cannot use. The fix above did nothing until that was
corrected. The oracle asks whether the step was replayed, not whether
it is prefill, so it stays engine-agnostic.

Also drops the multi-graph interpolation added earlier. Four decode
graphs across a context range moved the held-out error from -3.7% to
-1.5% and covering the range made no further difference, so it was
not worth its complexity. One graph per kind.
