Record the systematic optimism of priced kernels as its own problem

A kernel measured alone comes out cheaper than the same kernel in a
step, and it happens to every kernel measured so far. Documented as
open problem 21 with the ceiling it puts on item 1, since it bounds
what per-operator pricing can deliver however good the coverage
gets.

The profiler-free statement is the strong one: priced kernels sum to
2.332 ms against a 3.115 ms replayed step at 98.8% coverage, a ratio
of 0.749, with no instrument beyond the engine's own clock. Per
kernel, seven across two families, memory-bound and compute-bound,
the ratio clusters at 0.83 to 0.93.

Whether it is constant is the question that decides the fix. Constant
means one calibration factor and the mechanism can stay unexplained;
varying by kernel class means a factor is wrong. Seven kernels at one
batch size cannot tell those apart, and widening the sample is mostly
bookkeeping -- every priced signature already has an in-situ
counterpart in a profile of the same workload.

Also records what it is not, each ruled out by measurement: not cache
residency (rotation plateaus), not attention-specific (the gemms do
it too and never touch the KV cache), not overlap (per-call cost
asymptotes), not instrument disagreement (ablation and profile agree
to 1%). And the caveat that the per-kernel column carries 5-10% of
profiler inflation which the 0.749 does not.
