Probe interpolating prices across shapes, and rule it out

The cheap candidate for 6b was empirical/interpolated at operator
level: price an operator at a few shapes and interpolate, rather than
derive a graph per shape. One gemm from the 0.6B decode graph, priced
at 20 values of M from 1 to 1024.

It looks like it works. Median error 3-8% from as few as four
measured shapes, and 2.6% within the bracketed range. Every worst-case
column is one point: the price jumps from 58.07us at M=768 to 137.45
at M=1024 where the trend says 77.

The cliff is a kernel change and the price list already recorded it --
MT128x1 at 256-384, MT256x1 at 512-768, MT256x2 at 1024. So the
obvious guard is to interpolate only between neighbours that ran the
same kernel, and that guard refuses almost everything: the library
re-tunes at nearly every shape, twelve tile configurations across
twenty values of M, so 8 of 10 held-out shapes have no same-kernel
bracket. The two that do come out at 0.4%.

So interpolation is not sound-but-imprecise. It is accurate most of
the time with no way to tell when it is not, and for a tool whose
output is a ranking of configurations an occasional silent 60% is
worse than a uniform 10%.

What survives: decode shapes are the capture ladder, eleven rungs known
from config before anything runs, so they can be measured rather than
interpolated -- empirical/measured over an enumerable set. Prefill
token counts are arbitrary, so prefill still needs empirical/fitted
per operator against M, or derivation. That splits 6b into a bounded
piece and an open one.
