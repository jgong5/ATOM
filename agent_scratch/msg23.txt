Align the cost-modelling vocabulary on two questions

The taxonomy in core/cost/__init__.py described a design that was
never built. It defined calibrated as "the analytical shape scaled by
a measured efficiency factor" -- calibrated has no analytical
component and never did -- and gave the genus name empirical to one
species of it, while priced did not appear at all.

Two questions replace it.

What is costed: a model step, the forward for a step of a given shape;
or serving -- admission, scheduling, KV cache management. Serving
decisions are not costed because they are not modelled: Compass runs
ATOM's real scheduler and replaces only the forward, so what serving
contributes is the time it consumes, not the choices it makes.

How the cost is obtained: analytical, computed without measuring the
subject; or empirical, derived from measuring it. Empirical is a genus
written empirical/<species>:

  empirical/measured      the unit itself is timed -- priced
  empirical/fitted        a form chosen, coefficients regressed -- calibrated
  empirical/interpolated  no form assumed, neighbours looked up

Those are the species built so far and not a closed set;
empirical/extrapolated and others are equally admissible, which is the
point of naming the genus separately.

EmpiricalCostOracle is renamed InterpolatedCostOracle, since it claimed
the genus while being one species: every oracle here except constant
is empirical. Each oracle's docstring now opens with its own
genus/species.

Step-level and operator-level are dropped as a taxonomy. priced reaches
a model step by summing operators and calibrated by fitting the step
directly, which is a difference of method rather than of subject --
both answer what one step costs.

Nothing analytical exists yet for cost. The word is reserved rather
than aspirational; the memory-budget code already uses it in the same
sense.
