Complete the parallelism sweep, and find the per-launch constant is
not group-size independent

Qwen3.8-27B at TP=1, 2 and 4, both constants carried from the 0.6B:

  TP    ttft     tpot   latency
   1  -2.58%   +0.94%   -0.90%
   2  +2.45%   +0.00%   +1.37%
   4  +6.46%   -5.73%   +1.37%

TP=1 and TP=2 sit inside a few percent. TP=4 comes apart in a way that
names its cause: decode is 5.73% low, priced kernels plus 655 launches
at 2.25us giving 11.690ms against 12.401 measured, and closing it needs
3.33us a launch. The same 2.25us was right at TP=1, TP=2, and on a
dense 0.6B, a 27B hybrid and a 30B MoE.

What changes is the group. A decode graph holds 129 all_reduce_ per
rank, and a collective's boundary cost includes waiting for the
slowest peer, which has more chances to be slow as the group grows. So
the constant is model-independent and not group-size independent, and
a graph whose collectives are a sixth of its launches is where that
shows. Testable: fit it separately for collective and non-collective
launches, which OpSpec.group already distinguishes.

Also fixes a deadlock that stopped TP=4 pricing entirely. The run died
in a distributed recvBytes after CUDA graph capture -- when the
benchmark runs -- with no error of its own. The per-signature kernel
breakdown makes two extra calls per signature and for a collective
those are two extra collectives, so a breakdown that fails on one rank
and not another diverges the group silently. Off by default under
parallelism now. Anything optional in the pricing loop has to be
optional on every rank or on none.

On problem 4, which recorded an unexplained 19% calibrate-vs-evaluate
gap on TP=4 prefill: the priced oracle gets TTFT to +6.46% on the same
configuration, which locates most of that 19% in the calibrated oracle
rather than in the machine.
