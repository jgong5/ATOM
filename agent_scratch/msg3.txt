Correct the overlap claim: the captured calls serialise

I attributed the benchmark's remaining 30% to graph-mode overlap.
That was wrong, and this session had already established the
opposite once. Two measurements refute it.

Profiling the real run instead of differencing it resolves the
operator into three kernels, over 896 launches (28 layers x 32
decode steps):

                      in situ   benchmark
    attention        11.07 us     7.70 us
    rope              9.04 us     6.87 us
    KV write          6.33 us     5.37 us
    operator         26.44 us    19.94 us

26.44 against the ablation's 26.1 -- two methods sharing no
machinery, 1% apart. Every kernel is cheaper in the benchmark by
15-30%, which is not what one pathological kernel looks like and not
what overlap looks like either.

The batch sweep settles it directly. Fitting measured = c + r/B to
B=1 and B=64 gives r=6.1us, c=18.2us, which then predicts B=4 as
19.76 (measured 19.93) and B=16 as 18.61 (18.71). A constant
per-replay overhead amortised over a flat per-call cost. Per-call
cost asymptotes; concurrency would keep driving it down.

What is left is cache residency: the benchmark replays against one
layer's KV cache 64 times back to back, where a real step touches 28
layers' regions once each amid the other ~300 kernels. Recorded as a
hypothesis with evidence, not a demonstration, together with the
experiment that would settle it -- rotate layer_name across the
captured calls. Neither hot nor cold mode says anything about this,
because _build_arg_sets rotates arguments and the KV cache is not an
argument.

Also corrects the aten::slice claim: falling 315x is a view being
elided, not a kernel overlapping.
