Cool the KV cache the benchmark was pricing warm

The cache is not an argument -- attention fetches it from the forward
context -- so no argument-rotation mode reaches it, and hot and cold
alike priced it resident across the whole captured batch. Since
Compass now installs that context, the fix goes there: install
returns one thunk per KV region, offsetting the recorded call's block
ids and slots by the span it used, and _time_in_graph installs the
i-th before capturing the i-th call. Host-only work, so nothing is
allocated inside the capture, and a region is revisited only after
every other has been walked -- eviction reproduced rather than
simulated. Scrubbing instead would mean a 100us baseline subtraction
against an 18us operator.

The sweep shows the mechanism: 18.31us at one region, rising to
20.77 at 128 and flat to 512 (2.6 GB). One region per captured call
is the default, which is where the knee is.

It closes a third of the gap -- 25.21us in situ against 21.06 now,
19.94 before -- and the error goes 27% to 18%.

The rest was never attention's. The gemms in the same graph are
priced against their own in-situ kernels at 39.19us versus 44.79us,
0.875, and they never touch the KV cache. Every kernel is priced
13-16% under what it costs in a step, whatever it touches. That is a
property of pricing kernels apart from the step and no cache
modelling will remove it. Two candidates recorded, one of which this
harness has already half-measured: cold came out 0.94x hot, read at
the time as independent output buffers pipelining where a shared one
serialises.

Worth knowing because it bounds the approach: priced kernels are
systematically optimistic by about an eighth, before any question of
whether they sum to a step.
