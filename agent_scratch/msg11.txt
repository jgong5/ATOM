Price collectives, and validate the op-graph oracle at TP=2

The open question was whether a microbenchmark can price a
collective. It can: called in the worker, where the process group is
live, all_reduce_ prices at 10.38us for a [4,1024] decode and 71.33us
for a [1256,1024] prefill, and coverage comes to 98.3% on both ranks.
Only c10d::broadcast_ fails, taking a ProcessGroup object no JSON
artifact can hold, and it is one operator per step.

What makes it safe is pricing the union of every rank's graphs rather
than each rank's own. A collective needs all ranks calling it in
lockstep, so signature lists differing by one entry deadlock rather
than fail -- a hang with no error, inside the worker. The union is
identical across ranks by construction.

The graph and the prices then transfer to TP=2 unchanged. Only the two
overhead constants move:

              tp1 consts   refitted   real(n=3)   noise
  ttft            -5.8%      -2.2%    63.822ms   +-2.9%
  tpot            +6.2%      +2.4%     3.290ms   +-3.9%
  latency         +1.6%      +0.6%   165.805ms   +-2.2%

Reused from TP=1 they cost a few percent and pull in opposite
directions -- tpot over, ttft under -- which is what identified them
rather than the prices as the problem. Refitted: 1.97us/launch against
2.25, and 92.5us/op against 86.35. Seven and twelve percent off, in
different directions, on the same hardware and model.

So the constants are a property of a deployment, and problem 21's hope
of one calibration factor would have been wrong even had the ratio
been constant. Predicting a configuration nobody has run needs either
one measured step from it to fit two numbers, or an accepted ~5% error
on metrics whose overhead term is a quarter of the step.

Admission carries over untouched: 12.89ms at TP=2 against 13.19 at
TP=1, being engine-side work that never reaches a device.
