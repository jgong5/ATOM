Measure the decode ladder, and close the decode half of 6b

Decode shapes are the rungs of the capture ladder, known from config
before anything runs, so they are measured rather than interpolated --
which is what the interpolation probe concluded. Traced at seven rungs
by running seven workloads at 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32 and 64 concurrent
requests: the batch is the rung, so no shape rewriting and nothing to
get silently wrong. Priced as a union at 98.8%.

Two facts worth having on their own. The structure is rung-independent
-- 330 operators and 19 distinct kinds at every rung, one identical
multiset -- which is what makes this a shape family rather than seven
models. And capture_bucket matched the rung exactly in all seven runs,
so the key the oracle selects on is the one the engine used.

Against measured decode steps, with one constant:

  rung  measured    ladder   one graph
     1   2.848ms     +6.8%      +10.0%
     2   3.222ms     -4.7%       -2.8%
     4   3.133ms      0.0%        0.0%

    16   3.442ms     -1.2%       -9.0%
    32   4.119ms     -2.5%      -23.9%
    64   5.030ms     +1.3%      -37.7%
              median  2.1%        9.0%

The right column is what the oracle did before: one decode graph
traced at batch 4 answering for every rung. Fine near its own shape and
-37.7% at rung 64, and a served workload spends half its decode steps
at batch 64.

The oracle now selects by rung and refuses to interpolate: an
unmeasured rung falls back to the largest measured one and says so
once, which is wrong by a known mechanism rather than a silent one.

Also corrects an overstatement in the previous commit. The plain
per-launch constant is stable across the group, not across models: the
27B fits 1.82us where the 0.6B fits 2.25, both at TP=1 with no
collectives. The ladder gives seven estimates for the 0.6B spanning
1.53 to 2.43 with median 2.04, so the model-to-model difference sits
inside one model's scatter and the constant is known to about +-20%.
